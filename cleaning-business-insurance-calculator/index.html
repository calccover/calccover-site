How Much Does Cleaning Business Insurance Cost?

For most independent cleaning businesses and maid services operating in the United States, a standard insurance package ranges from $1,200 to $3,500 per year. The exact price depends heavily on whether you primarily service residential homes or large commercial office buildings. General liability insurance—which protects against slips, falls, and property damage caused by your crew—typically forms the core of your policy and starts around $800 to $1,500 annually for a standard $1,000,000 occurrence limit.

If you employ staff, mandatory workers' compensation insurance will add significantly to your overhead, calculated as a rate per $100 of your total payroll using standard industry class codes. Additionally, janitorial surety bonds and inland marine coverage for stolen or damaged customer property or cleaning equipment usually add another $150 to $500 per year, providing essential peace of mind for your clients.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

Insurance underwriters look at several specific risk characteristics when quoting a cleaning contractor. Your gross annual revenue serves as the primary exposure base for general liability; higher billings imply more hours worked and greater customer interaction. The type of cleaning you perform also matters deeply—commercial facilities often involve heavy machinery, wax strippers, and multi-story spaces, which can elevate liability exposure compared to basic residential tidying.

Your geographic location plays a critical role due to regional litigation trends and state-mandated workers' compensation fee schedules. Finally, your claims history is paramount. Businesses with clean loss runs over the past three to five years qualify for preferred tier pricing, whereas frequent prior property damage or employee injury claims will trigger surcharges or restrict your choice of admitted insurance carriers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is general liability insurance required by law for cleaners?

While most states do not legally mandate general liability insurance to operate a cleaning service, virtually all commercial property managers and a vast majority of residential clients require proof of insurance before handing over keys or awarding cleaning contracts.

Do I need workers' compensation if I am a sole proprietor?

In most states, single-owner cleaning businesses without employees are legally exempt from purchasing workers' compensation. However, many commercial clients still demand a waiver or proof of coverage, and some general contractors will require it even for independent subcontractors.

What is the difference between a surety bond and liability insurance?

General liability insurance covers third-party bodily injury and property damage claims. A surety bond, specifically a janitorial bond, reimburses your clients if an employee commits theft or larceny while inside their home or office building.
