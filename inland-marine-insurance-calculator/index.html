How Much Does Inland Marine Insurance Cost?

Inland marine insurance premiums typically range from $500 to over $3,000 annually for small to mid-sized businesses, heavily dependent on the total insured value of your equipment and goods in transit. Businesses that insure specialized heavy machinery or extensive commercial truck cargo face higher baseline rates due to increased loss severities. For standard contractors carrying standard tool sets valued under $50,000, basic policies often sit on the lower end of the pricing spectrum.

Your ultimate cost is determined primarily by applying a specific rate per $100 of insured value. When you scale your coverage limits to match high-value job site assets or cross-country inventory shipments, your premium adjusts directly. Working with carriers who specialize in your specific vertical can uncover package discounts when bundling general liability with inland marine floaters.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

Industry risk classification plays a massive role in determining your rate per $100 of value. Construction and heavy contracting businesses face elevated theft and damage hazards on open job sites compared to tech consultants transporting portable diagnostic gear. Consequently, construction rate factors sit significantly higher than professional service brackets.

Transit exposure is another core rating variable. Hauling goods across national highways introduces severe risks compared to storing tools securely in a locked commercial warehouse overnight. Deductible choices, equipment security measures like GPS tracking devices, and historical loss frequency further dictate whether your final policy leans toward the minimum $0.30 rate or approaches the maximum $1.20 rate per $100 of coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does inland marine insurance cover?

It covers property that is mobile, in transit, or stored off-premises. This includes contractor's tools, installation floaters, electronic data processing equipment, and commercial cargo moving via truck, rail, or air.

Is inland marine insurance required by law?

Unlike commercial auto liability, inland marine is rarely mandated by state statute. However, project owners, general contractors, or equipment leasing companies frequently require proof of inland marine coverage before allowing you on site.

Does standard general liability cover my tools and equipment?

Standard general liability policies generally exclude damage to or theft of your own tools, equipment, and inventory while away from your primary business location. You need an inland marine floater to properly protect these mobile assets.
