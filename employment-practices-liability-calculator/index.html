How Much Does Employment Practices Liability Insurance Cost?

Employment Practices Liability Insurance (EPLI) protects business owners against allegations of wrongful termination, discrimination, sexual harassment, and other employment-related torts. For most small to mid-sized businesses, annual premiums typically range from a few thousand dollars to over fifteen thousand dollars. The exact cost heavily depends on your organization's head count, risk controls, and the industry in which you operate.

While baseline coverage for a small office with ten employees might be relatively inexpensive, companies with larger workforces or those operating in states with strict employee-protection legislation will see higher pricing. Investing in robust human resources policies, formal handbooks, and management training can often qualify your business for lower premium tiers during the underwriting process.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

Underwriters evaluate multiple risk metrics when determining your final EPLI quotation. The primary driver is your total employee count, as more workers statistically correlate with a higher volume of potential workplace disputes. Part-time, seasonal, and full-time employees are all factored into the overall exposure calculation, though underwriters sometimes weigh them differently.

Geographic location is another vital rating factor. States like California and New York feature unique labor laws, higher average settlement amounts, and more active plaintiff attorney networks, driving up regional base rates. Additionally, your industry classification matters: sectors with high turnover or stringent regulatory environments, such as healthcare and hospitality, often face steeper underwriting multipliers than standard professional services firms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does EPLI insurance typically cover?

EPLI policies cover legal defense fees, settlements, and judgments arising from employee lawsuits alleging discrimination, wrongful termination, sexual harassment, breach of employment contract, and failure to promote.

Is third-party discrimination covered under standard EPLI?

Standard policies focus primarily on employee-on-employee claims. However, many modern commercial forms can be endorsed to include third-party liability covering claims made by customers, vendors, or visitors against your staff.

How can my business lower its EPLI premiums?

Insurers reward proactive risk management. Maintaining an up-to-date employee handbook, establishing clear anti-harassment reporting procedures, providing regular manager training, and consulting legal counsel before terminations will help reduce quotes.
