Home Services Insurance: Complete 2026 Guide

Home services businesses operate in client homes and businesses, which creates unique liability exposures. This hub covers insurance for cleaning companies, landscapers, and related trades.

By Emiliano · Last updated 2026-10-16 · 10 min read

$1,200 – $5,000
Typical annual cost for a small home services business

What insurance does a home services insurance business need?

Most home services insurance businesses need a stack of coverages. The priority list:

  • General Liability — Property damage to client homes or businesses, third-party injuries at job sites.
  • Workers' Compensation — Employee injuries (slips, strains, chemical exposure). Mandatory with W-2 employees.
  • Commercial Auto — Vehicles used for business purposes (equipment transport, client travel). Personal auto excludes business use.
  • Inland Marine — Tools and equipment in transit between job sites. Standard property policies exclude off-premises equipment.
  • Bonding — Some commercial cleaning and property management contracts require a surety bond.
  • Commercial Umbrella — Excess liability protection for high-exposure work (tree trimming, pressure washing).

Frequently asked questions

How much does cleaning business insurance cost?

Cleaning business insurance costs $1,200–$5,000 per year depending on residential vs. commercial work, number of employees, and state. A solo residential cleaner pays $800–$2,500; a commercial cleaning company with crews pays $2,000–$5,000+.

Do cleaning businesses need bonding?

Not legally, but many commercial cleaning contracts require a surety bond. Bonding covers client losses from theft or damage by your employees. Costs run $100–$500 per year for a $10,000–$25,000 bond.

What insurance does a landscaper need?

Landscapers need GL, workers' comp, commercial auto, and inland marine (for tools and equipment in transit). Total cost is $2,000–$5,000 per year for a small operation.

Do home services businesses need workers' comp?

Yes, in almost every state once you hire your first W-2 employee, part-time included. Cleaning and landscaping class codes run $1.50–$4.50 per $100 of payroll depending on state and specific work.

Is personal auto insurance enough for my work van?

No. Personal auto excludes business use. If you drive to client sites, transport equipment, or use your vehicle for any business purpose, you need commercial auto. Even a work van used only for commuting to job sites typically needs commercial coverage.

How can I lower my home services insurance cost?

Five levers: (1) verify your NCCI class code, (2) maintain clean driving records for all drivers, (3) bundle coverage in a BOP (10–25% savings), (4) install tracking equipment and telematics, (5) shop 3+ carriers every year.
