How Much Does Restaurant Insurance Cost?

Restaurant insurance costs between $2,500 and $15,000 per year for most establishments. A small cafe might pay $2,500–$4,000. A full-service restaurant with 60+ seats typically pays $6,000–$12,000. Bars and nightclubs pay the most due to higher liability exposure.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

1. Restaurant Type

Fast food and cafes have the lowest rates. Full-service restaurants cost more due to kitchen operations and alcohol service. Bars and nightclubs pay the highest rates because of liquor liability.

2. Number of Seats

More seats means more customers and more exposure. Each additional seat increases the premium roughly linearly.

3. Annual Revenue

Higher revenue means higher coverage limits are typically required, which increases the premium. Insurers use revenue as a proxy for business size and risk.

4. Alcohol Service

If you serve alcohol, your premium increases significantly because of liquor liability exposure. A full liquor license can double your insurance costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is restaurant insurance required?

Yes in most cases. General liability is often required by landlords. Workers comp is required in most states if you have employees. Liquor liability is required by law if you serve alcohol.

What does restaurant insurance cover?

A typical policy bundles general liability, property insurance (equipment, inventory, furniture), business interruption (lost income from closures), workers comp, and liquor liability if applicable. Food spoilage coverage is also common.

How can I lower my premium?

Maintain a clean claims history, implement food safety training, install fire suppression systems, and choose a higher deductible. Bundling with a Business Owners Policy (BOP) can save 15–25%.
