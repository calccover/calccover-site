How much does commercial auto insurance cost?

Commercial auto insurance costs $1,400–$3,000 per year for a passenger car or light pickup truck, and $4,500–$6,500+ per year for box trucks and heavy trucks. That's per vehicle, and it reflects the higher risk of severe property damage and bodily injury that larger commercial vehicles present.

Fleet discounts change the math significantly. Insurers reward businesses with 2+ commercial vehicles on one policy:

  • 2–4 vehicles: 8% discount
  • 5–9 vehicles: 15% discount
  • 10+ vehicles: 20% discount

State and metro location adds 25–45% to the base rate. Our 50-state cost analysis shows commercial auto varies 2x between the cheapest state (Maine at $3,298/year) and the most expensive (New York at $7,996/year) for the same coverage.

What factors affect your commercial auto premium?

Insurance underwriters evaluate several core risk metrics when establishing commercial auto premiums:

  • Vehicle classification and weight — larger vehicles inflict greater damage in collisions, driving up liability exposure
  • Radius of operation — local service (under 50 miles) pays less than long-haul interstate transit
  • Driver history and MVRs — the single biggest rate driver. Every employed driver's motor vehicle record is reviewed for accidents, speeding tickets, and major violations.
  • Garaging location — regional litigation climate, traffic density, and weather-related loss trends shape final pricing
  • Cargo type — hazardous materials, high-value electronics, and refrigerated goods require endorsements that add 20–40% to premium
  • Fleet size — 2+ vehicles on one policy triggers fleet discounts of 8–20%
  • Telematics adoption — carriers discount 5–15% for vehicles with GPS/dashcam telematics, because it improves driver accountability

What commercial auto coverages do you need?

Commercial auto policies bundle multiple coverages. Most businesses need all of these:

  • Liability (bodily injury + property damage) — required by law in almost every state. Standard limits are $1M combined single limit (CSL); many commercial contracts require higher.
  • Collision — covers damage to your own vehicles in an accident, regardless of fault.
  • Comprehensive — covers non-collision damage: theft, fire, weather, vandalism.
  • Hired and Non-Owned Auto (HNOA) — covers rentals and employee-owned vehicles used for business. Often bundled with a BOP or general liability policy.
  • Cargo insurance — for businesses hauling freight. Separate policy, not included in standard commercial auto.
  • Motor truck cargo — required for interstate trucking under federal regulations.

Frequently asked questions

How much does commercial auto insurance cost?

Commercial auto insurance costs $1,400–$3,000 per year for a passenger car or light pickup, and $4,500–$6,500+ per year for box trucks and heavy trucks. Fleet discounts of 8–20% apply at 2+ vehicles. High-risk states (New York, California) and metro areas add 25–45% to the base rate.

Is commercial auto insurance required for personal vehicles used for business?

Yes. Standard personal auto policies exclude coverage for accidents that occur while vehicles are used for commercial purposes — making deliveries, visiting job sites, or transporting tools and equipment. Some carriers offer a business-use endorsement on personal policies for light business use, but most businesses need a standalone commercial auto policy.

How can I lower my commercial auto insurance premiums?

Six levers: (1) maintain a clean driving record across all employed drivers — MVRs are the single biggest rate driver; (2) install telematics tracking (5–15% savings); (3) raise deductibles from $500 to $2,500 (10–15% savings); (4) bundle commercial auto with GL and workers' comp (10–25% savings); (5) qualify for fleet discounts at 2+ vehicles (8–20%); (6) shop 3+ carriers — rates vary 20–30% for identical coverage.

What limits of liability should my business carry?

Most commercial policies feature a combined single limit (CSL) starting at $1,000,000. Higher limits or commercial umbrella policies are strongly recommended for contractors, logistics companies, and businesses carrying valuable cargo or heavy machinery. Some commercial contracts and government work require $2M or higher.

Do I need commercial auto insurance if I drive my personal car for business?

Short answer: If you use your personal vehicle for any business purpose — client visits, deliveries, transporting tools, rideshare — your personal auto policy may deny a claim. Personal policies exclude business use in almost all cases.

Options: (1) a business-use endorsement on your personal policy for light use, or (2) a standalone commercial auto policy for regular business use.

What is a fleet discount and how do I qualify?

Short answer: A fleet discount is a percentage reduction on your commercial auto premium applied when you insure multiple vehicles on one policy. Most carriers start fleet discounts at 2 vehicles (8% savings), scale to 15% at 5 vehicles, and cap around 20% at 10+ vehicles.

To qualify, all vehicles must be on the same policy with the same carrier and meet minimum driver requirements.

Does commercial auto insurance cover the driver or the vehicle?

Short answer: Commercial auto insurance covers both — but the exact scope depends on the policy form. Standard commercial auto policies cover specific vehicles listed on the policy and any permissive driver operating them for business purposes.

If you use hired or non-owned vehicles (rentals, employee-owned cars used for work), you need Hired and Non-Owned Auto (HNOA) coverage.
