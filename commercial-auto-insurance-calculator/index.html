How Much Does Commercial Auto Insurance Cost?

Commercial auto insurance costs between $1,200 and $8,000 per year per vehicle for most businesses, depending on the vehicle type and use. Sedans and pickups used for sales or service calls typically cost $1,200–$2,500 annually. Box trucks and semis used for delivery or long-haul transport cost $4,000–$15,000 or more.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

1. Vehicle Type

Larger and heavier vehicles cost more to insure because they cause more damage in accidents. A sedan is cheapest; a semi truck is most expensive.

2. Primary Use

Vehicles used for long-haul transport have the highest rates because of miles driven and accident exposure. Service and sales vehicles have the lowest.

3. Number of Vehicles

Fleet discounts reduce the per-vehicle rate as your fleet grows. Insuring 5 vehicles usually costs less per vehicle than insuring 1.

4. State

States with higher accident rates and more expensive claims (California, New York) have higher premiums. Texas and other business-friendly states tend to be lower.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is commercial auto insurance required?

Yes, in almost every state. Any vehicle used for business purposes requires commercial auto coverage. Personal auto policies typically exclude business use, which means a claim could be denied if you're driving for work.

What does commercial auto cover?

It covers liability (bodily injury and property damage to others), physical damage to your vehicle, medical payments, and uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage. It can also include hired and non-owned auto coverage if employees drive rentals or their own vehicles for work.

How can I lower my premium?

Install telematics devices, maintain clean driving records, raise deductibles, and bundle with other business policies (like general liability) for a BOP discount. Safety training programs can also reduce premiums.
