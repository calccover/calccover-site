Commercial Insurance Cost in 2026: Complete Benchmarks by Industry

How much does commercial insurance cost? For a typical small business with 1–5 employees, expect to pay between $2,500 and $8,000 per year across general liability, commercial property, and workers' compensation. But that range hides enormous variation — a consultancy in North Dakota pays $2,500, while a roofing company in Hawaii pays $40,000+ for the same number of employees. This guide breaks down exactly what drives the number, with 2026 benchmarks for 40+ industries.

What drives the cost: 7 factors

1. Industry class code (biggest single factor)

Every business is assigned an NCCI class code that reflects its actual work. Class code is the base rate every carrier starts from. The spread is enormous: a clerical class code runs $0.30–$0.80 per $100 of payroll, while roofing runs $5.00–$12.00. Misclassification is the single most common reason small businesses overpay — sometimes by 30–50%.

2. State (40–60% swing)

The same coverage costs 40–60% more in the Northeast than in the Mountain West. Our 50-state analysis found workers' comp alone ranges from $0.50 per $100 of payroll in North Dakota to $2.52 in Hawaii. The variation is driven by statutory benefit levels, litigation climate, and whether the state operates a workers' comp monopoly.

3. Revenue and payroll (linear driver)

Most commercial policies scale with revenue and payroll. A business with $500K in payroll pays roughly 2x the workers' comp premium of a business with $250K in payroll in the same class code and state. Property insurance scales with insured value. GL scales more loosely but still responds to revenue.

4. Claims history / Ex-Mod (2x swing)

Your experience modification factor (Ex-Mod) is a multiplier based on your claims history over the past 3 years. A 0.85 Ex-Mod is a 15% credit. A 1.40 Ex-Mod is a 40% debit. A single serious workers' comp claim can raise your Ex-Mod for three years — turning a $5,000 annual premium into $7,000+ for the full period.

5. Coverage limits and deductibles

Going from $1M/$2M to $2M/$4M on general liability typically raises the premium 20–35%. Going from a $1,000 to a $2,500 deductible on GL cuts the premium 10–15%. Both levers are worth understanding before you buy.

6. Vehicle count and type (commercial auto)

Commercial auto is priced per vehicle per year, with wide spreads by vehicle type: a sedan used for consulting runs $1,800–$2,800 per year, while a semi-truck runs $12,000–$18,000 per year. Fleets of 5+ vehicles often qualify for fleet rating, which can cut per-vehicle cost 10–20%.

7. Carrier and market cycle

Identical coverage varies 20–30% between carriers in the same state. On top of that, the insurance market has hard and soft cycles. Since 2022, commercial insurance rates have increased 4–8% per year nationally on average, driven by inflation in repair costs, litigation trends, and reinsurance pricing.

How to lower your commercial insurance cost

Six legitimate levers, ranked by impact:

1. Verify your NCCI class code

Ask your broker to confirm your class code in writing. Misclassification is common and expensive. A plumber misclassified as a general contractor can pay 40% more than necessary. A marketing consultant misclassified as a "business services" catch-all can pay 30% more than their actual professional services class code requires.

Potential savings: 10–40%

2. Shop 3+ carriers every year

Rates for identical coverage vary 20–30% between carriers. Renewal loyalty is expensive. Set a calendar reminder 60 days before renewal to get at least 3 competitive quotes.

Potential savings: 15–30%

3. Raise your deductibles

Moving from a $500 to a $2,500 deductible on property and GL typically reduces annual premium 10–15%. If you can absorb the higher deductible in the event of a claim, this is one of the cleanest ways to reduce cost.

Potential savings: 10–15%

4. Bundle into a BOP

If you qualify for a Business Owner's Policy, bundling GL and property typically saves 10–25% versus buying them separately. Most BOPs also include business income coverage automatically.

Potential savings: 10–25%

5. Improve your claims history

Every claim you file affects your Ex-Mod for 3 years. Paying small claims out of pocket (when the amount is under 2–3% of premium) is often cheaper than the resulting premium increase. Implement safety protocols that reduce the frequency and severity of claims.

Potential savings: 10–40% (compounds over 3 years)

6. Pay annually instead of monthly

Installment fees run 3–8% of annual premium. If you have the cash flow, paying annually eliminates those fees.

Potential savings: 3–8%

Frequently asked questions

How much does commercial insurance cost per month?

For a typical small business with 1–5 employees, commercial insurance runs $200–$700 per month total across GL, property, and workers' comp. A one-person LLC with no employees and a low-risk industry can pay as little as $40–$80 per month for general liability alone. A construction company with 10 employees and multiple vehicles can pay $2,000–$5,000 per month.

What is the cheapest commercial insurance for a small business?

A standalone general liability policy is the cheapest commercial coverage for most small businesses, averaging $40–$80 per month for a $1M/$2M limit in a low-risk industry. A BOP (Business Owner's Policy) bundling GL and property typically runs $60–$200 per month. Workers' comp is the most expensive line for businesses with employees, averaging $0.50–$2.52 per $100 of payroll.

Why is commercial insurance so expensive?

Three factors drive most premium costs: (1) your industry class code — construction, trucking, and healthcare pay 2–5x more than offices and retail; (2) your state — the same coverage costs 40–60% more in the Northeast than in the Mountain West; (3) your claims history — a single serious claim can raise premiums 20–45% for three years.

Does commercial insurance cost more in certain states?

Yes. Workers' comp rates alone range from $0.50 per $100 of payroll in North Dakota to $2.52 in Hawaii — a 5x spread. General liability varies 3–4x across states. New York is the most expensive state overall, ranking top 5 in workers' comp, general liability, and commercial auto.

Can I negotiate commercial insurance premiums?

Not directly with the carrier, but you can lower your premium through these levers: increasing deductibles (10–15% savings), paying annually instead of monthly (3–5% savings), bundling policies into a BOP (10–25% savings), improving your claims history, and shopping 3+ carriers (20–30% rate variance between carriers for identical coverage).

How much is workers' comp insurance for 5 employees?

A business with 5 employees and $250,000 in annual payroll pays $1,250 in North Dakota at $0.50 per $100, or $6,300 in Hawaii at $2.52 per $100 for a clerical class code. Rates climb sharply for high-risk class codes: a roofing contractor with the same payroll pays $12,500–$25,000 depending on the state.

Do I need to pay the full annual premium upfront?

No. Most carriers offer monthly payment plans with 10–25% down and 9–11 monthly installments. The installment fee is typically 3–8% of the annual premium, which is worth paying to preserve cash flow for most small businesses. Some carriers offer 12-pay with no interest for qualified businesses.

Why did my commercial insurance premium go up this year?

Common reasons: (1) your revenue or payroll grew, increasing exposure; (2) your industry filed rate increases with the state; (3) your experience modification factor rose after a claim; (4) the carrier tightened underwriting in your state; (5) inflation raised the cost to repair or replace insured property. On average, commercial insurance premiums have increased 4–8% per year nationally since 2022.

How do I know if I'm overpaying for commercial insurance?

Three tests: (1) compare your rate against state benchmarks in this article — if you're 20%+ above the average for your industry and state, you're likely overpaying; (2) get 3+ quotes from competing carriers (rates for identical coverage vary 20–30%); (3) verify your NCCI class code — misclassification is the single most common reason small businesses overpay, sometimes by 30–50%.

Does commercial insurance cost less if I work from home?

Yes, but not as much as you might expect. A home-based business insurance policy bundling GL and limited business property typically runs $400–$1,200 per year, compared to $600–$2,500 for a BOP with a commercial location. The savings come mostly from lower property values (no commercial building) and reduced foot traffic (fewer visitor injuries).
