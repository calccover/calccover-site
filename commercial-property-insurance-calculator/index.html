How much does commercial property insurance cost?

Commercial property insurance costs $1,000–$3,000 per year for most small to mid-sized businesses covering $500,000–$1,000,000 in total insured value. That works out to roughly $0.30–$0.80 per $100 of insured value, depending on property type, construction class, location, and occupancy.

Businesses operating in high-risk geographic zones or using older, combustible construction materials pay significantly more. A wood-frame retail building in Florida can pay $1.00+ per $100 of value; a fire-resistive office building in Ohio can pay $0.30.

What factors affect your commercial property premium?

Insurers evaluate several core underwriting characteristics when determining commercial property quotes:

  • Total Insured Value (TIV) — combines the replacement cost of your building structure and the value of your business contents, inventory, and equipment. Premium scales linearly above $500K.
  • Construction type — fire-resistive steel and concrete pay the lowest rates; wood-frame buildings pay 30–50% more.
  • Geographic location — coastal and disaster-prone states (Florida, Texas, California) carry the highest rate multipliers due to catastrophe exposure.
  • Occupancy type — restaurants and manufacturing pay more than offices due to fire and equipment risk.
  • Building age — pre-1980 buildings typically pay 15–25% more due to outdated systems and construction.
  • Protection class — buildings near fire hydrants and fire stations with professional fire departments get lower rates.
  • Claims history — clean loss runs qualify for preferred pricing tiers.

What's covered (and what's not)

Covered: building structure, outdoor fixtures, business personal property (furniture, equipment, inventory, computers), tenant improvements, signs, fences. Perils covered include fire, lightning, windstorm, hail, explosion, riot, vandalism, theft, and sprinkler leakage.

Not covered by standard commercial property:

  • Flood — requires separate flood policy (NFIP or private)
  • Earthquake — requires separate earthquake endorsement
  • Business interruption — requires endorsement or a BI policy
  • Employee dishonesty / theft — requires crime coverage
  • Equipment breakdown — requires equipment breakdown endorsement
  • Data / cyber — requires cyber liability policy

Frequently asked questions

What is covered under commercial property insurance?

Commercial property insurance generally covers your building structure, outdoor fixtures, office furniture, equipment, inventory, and computers against designated perils like fire, theft, wind damage, hail, and vandalism. Coverage extends to tenant improvements you made to a leased space, signage, and fences subject to policy sublimits.

Is business interruption insurance included?

Standard commercial property policies focus exclusively on physical assets. Business interruption or loss of income coverage is usually purchased as an endorsement or is automatically included in a Business Owner's Policy (BOP). Adding BI coverage typically raises premium 15–25% but protects you if a covered loss forces a temporary shutdown.

What is actual cash value versus replacement cost?

Short answer: Replacement cost coverage pays to rebuild or replace damaged property with new materials of like kind and quality without deduction for depreciation. Actual cash value factors in depreciation, paying only what the item was worth at the time of loss.

Replacement cost costs 15–30% more in premium but avoids a coverage gap at claim time — especially important for older buildings and equipment.

How much does commercial property insurance cost?

Most small to mid-sized businesses pay $1,000–$3,000 per year for $500,000–$1,000,000 in total insured value. Rates are roughly $0.30–$0.80 per $100 of insured value, depending on property type, construction, location, and occupancy.

Do I need commercial property insurance if I lease my space?

Yes. Your landlord's policy covers the building structure only — not your business personal property, tenant improvements, or equipment. Commercial leases almost universally require tenants to carry property insurance on their contents, fixtures, and improvements.

What construction type gets the cheapest commercial property insurance?

Fire-resistive construction (steel and concrete) pays the lowest rates. Non-combustible construction pays second-lowest. Joisted masonry is mid-tier. Frame construction (wood) pays the highest rates — 30–50% more than fire-resistive for the same building.
