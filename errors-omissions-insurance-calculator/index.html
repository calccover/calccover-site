How Much Does Errors & Omissions Insurance Cost?

Errors and omissions (E&O) insurance, also known as professional liability insurance, typically costs small to mid-sized service firms anywhere from $1,000 to $5,000 per year for a standard $1,000,000 policy. However, pricing varies significantly based on your industry's inherent litigation exposure, contract sizes, and operational scope. Sole proprietors or independent contractors with minimal revenue often secure basic coverage on the lower end of the spectrum, whereas large consulting firms or engineering practices handling high-stakes projects can see premiums climb substantially.

Understanding these cost parameters helps business owners budget accurately when responding to client requests for proof of insurance or when bidding on enterprise-level master services agreements. Investing in a robust E&O policy protects your balance sheet from catastrophic legal defense costs and client lawsuits alleging financial loss due to professional negligence, missed deadlines, or substandard deliverables.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

Insurance underwriters evaluate multiple operational and financial variables when pricing professional liability coverage. Your primary industry dictates the baseline risk profile, as certain fields like engineering, accounting, and legal services carry higher severity claims than general marketing or creative consulting. Annual revenue serves as a direct indicator of your business volume and project exposure; higher billings imply a greater number of active clients and larger potential damages if an error occurs.

Additionally, your total headcount and the experience level of your staff influence underwriting decisions, since more employees generally mean an increased chance of human error during service delivery. Finally, your chosen coverage limits, policy deductibles, and geographic operating region play major roles in determining your ultimate premium, as states with higher historical litigation rates often carry upward pricing adjustments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Errors and Omissions insurance actually cover?

E&O insurance covers financial losses suffered by your clients due to your actual or alleged professional negligence, errors, omissions, or failure to deliver services as promised in your contract. This includes legal defense fees, court costs, and settlements or judgments.

Is E&O insurance legally required for my business?

In most jurisdictions, E&O insurance is not mandated by state law for general business operations. However, corporate clients, government agencies, and industry licensing boards frequently require proof of professional liability insurance before awarding contracts or issuing credentials.

How does E&O differ from General Liability insurance?

General Liability insurance protects your business against third-party bodily injury and property damage, such as a client slipping in your office. E&O insurance specifically covers financial losses resulting from professional mistakes, advice, or failure to perform services.
