How Much Does Contractor Insurance Cost?

Contractor insurance costs between $1,500 and $12,000 per year for most trades. General liability alone runs $1,000–$4,000 annually. Adding workers comp, commercial auto, and tools coverage can push the total to $8,000–$15,000 for established contractors with employees.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

1. Trade Type

Roofing is the most expensive trade to insure because of fall risk and high claim frequency. Electrical and plumbing are moderate. Painting and landscaping are on the lower end.

2. Annual Revenue

Higher revenue means larger projects and more exposure. Premiums scale roughly linearly with revenue for the first $1M, then increase more slowly.

3. Employees and Subcontractors

Each employee adds workers comp exposure. Subcontractors can increase your premium significantly if they aren't independently insured — you may inherit their liability.

4. State

States with stricter workers comp laws (California, New York) charge more. Right-to-work states with less litigation (Texas, Florida) charge less.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is contractor insurance required?

Yes. Nearly every state requires general liability for licensed contractors. Most general contractors require their subcontractors to carry it too. Workers comp is required in almost all states if you have employees.

What does contractor insurance cover?

A typical package includes general liability (third-party injury/property damage), workers comp (employee injuries), commercial auto (vehicles), tools and equipment coverage, and builder's risk (for projects under construction).

How can I lower my premium?

Maintain a clean claims history, use insured subcontractors, implement safety training, and bundle policies. Raising your deductible from $1,000 to $5,000 can save 20–30%.
