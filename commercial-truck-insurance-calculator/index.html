How Much Does Commercial Truck Insurance Cost?

Commercial truck insurance costs between $8,000 and $20,000 per year for most owner-operators, depending on the state, cargo type, and driving record. Small fleets (2-5 trucks) typically pay $12,000 to $25,000 per year. These are averages — your actual premium depends on your specific operations.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

1. Business Type

Owner-operators with a single truck typically pay less than large fleets because they have lower total exposure. However, owner-operators often pay a higher rate per truck due to lack of fleet discounts.

2. Cargo Type

Hauling general freight is the cheapest option. Refrigerated and flatbed cargo add moderate premiums. Hazmat hauling is the most expensive — premiums can be 50-100% higher than general freight.

3. State Regulations

States with higher insurance requirements and more accidents (like California and Texas) typically have higher premiums. States with lower minimum requirements may offer lower rates.

4. Years in Business

New authorities (less than 2 years) pay significantly higher premiums — often 20-40% more than established carriers with a clean record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is commercial truck insurance required?

Yes. Federal law requires commercial trucks to carry a minimum of $750,000 in liability coverage for interstate operations. Many states have their own minimum requirements for intrastate carriers.

What does commercial truck insurance cover?

Standard coverage includes liability (bodily injury and property damage), cargo insurance (protects the freight you haul), physical damage (covers your truck), and non-trucking liability (for when you're not under dispatch).

How can I lower my premium?

The biggest factors are your driving record, years in business, and safety practices. Installing dash cams, maintaining a clean MVR, and completing safety training courses can reduce your premium by 10-20%.

Do I need an MC number to get insurance?

Yes. Insurance carriers require an active MC number and DOT number before they will issue a commercial trucking policy. If you're a new authority, expect higher rates until you build a claims history.
