How much does E&O insurance cost?

Errors and omissions insurance costs $500–$3,000 per year for most small professional services businesses. That covers $1M per claim / $2M aggregate — the standard limits required by most client contracts.

The rate varies dramatically by profession. A marketing consultant might pay $600/year; a financial advisor pays $2,000–$8,000 for the same limits. The gap reflects the underlying risk: financial advice carries far more liability exposure per dollar of revenue than marketing work does.

Three variables account for most of the spread:

  • Profession — financial services, insurance, and healthcare pay 3–10× what marketing and IT consulting pay
  • Revenue — premium scales roughly linearly above $500K in annual revenue
  • Claims history — a single E&O claim raises premiums 25–50% for 3 years

What drives E&O premium up or down

Beyond profession and state, insurers apply credits and debits during underwriting:

  • Revenue — premium scales roughly linearly above $500K. A firm at $5M revenue pays 5–8× a firm at $500K revenue.
  • Years in business — newer firms pay 10–20% more; established firms with 5+ years of clean history get preferred rates
  • Claims history — a single claim raises premiums 25–50% for 3 years; two claims can make you uninsurable with mainstream carriers
  • Deductible — $2,500 is standard. Moving to $5,000 saves ~10%; $10,000 saves ~20%
  • Coverage limit — moving from $1M to $2M roughly doubles the limit but only adds 30–50% to the premium (limits are not priced linearly)
  • Contract quality — strong engagement contracts with limitation-of-liability clauses can earn 5–10% credits
  • Bundling — bundling E&O with GL, cyber, or BOP can save 10–15%

How to lower your E&O insurance cost

  1. Raise your deductible. Moving from $2,500 to $5,000 typically saves 10%; $10,000 saves 20%. Only do this if you have the cash reserve to cover the higher deductible.
  2. Shop 3+ carriers every year. Rates for identical coverage vary 20–30% between carriers. Loyalty is expensive in E&O — renewals are often priced above what a new carrier would offer.
  3. Use strong engagement contracts. Clear scope, limitation-of-liability clauses, and dispute resolution provisions reduce claims — and some carriers offer 5–10% credits for well-drafted contracts.
  4. Bundle with other coverage. E&O + GL + cyber bundled into a single policy saves 10–15%. See our complete BOP guide.
  5. Maintain a clean 3-year loss run. One claim raises premiums 25–50% for 3 years. Consider paying small claims out of pocket to preserve a clean loss run.

Frequently asked questions

How much does errors and omissions insurance cost?

Short answer: Most small professional services businesses pay $500–$3,000 per year for a $1M/$2M E&O policy. Real estate agents pay $500–$1,500; IT consultants $800–$2,500; management consultants $1,000–$3,000; insurance agents $1,200–$4,000; financial advisors $2,000–$8,000.

Premiums depend on profession, revenue, years in business, claims history, and deductible.

What is the average E&O premium for a small consulting firm?

A small management consulting firm with under $1M in revenue typically pays $1,000–$3,000 per year for $1M/$2M E&O coverage. IT consultants pay $800–$2,500. Marketing consultants pay $600–$1,800. Financial advisors pay significantly more ($2,000–$8,000) due to fiduciary liability exposure.

Do E&O rates vary by state?

Yes, but less dramatically than commercial property rates. E&O premiums vary 15–30% between the cheapest and most expensive states, compared to 3–4× for property insurance. The main state-level drivers are litigation climate, regulatory environment, and carrier competition.

California, New York, and Florida tend to have the highest E&O rates.

What factors affect my E&O premium?

Six factors drive E&O premiums: profession (financial advisors pay 3–5× more than marketing consultants), revenue (premium scales roughly linearly above $500K), years in business (newer firms pay 10–20% more), claims history (one claim raises premiums 25–50% for 3 years), deductible ($2,500 vs. $5,000 changes premium by 10–15%), and coverage limit ($2M vs. $1M doubles the limit but only adds 30–50% to the premium).

Is E&O insurance the same as professional liability insurance?

Yes — errors and omissions insurance and professional liability insurance are the same coverage. Both protect against claims of negligence, errors, or failure to perform professional services. The terms are used interchangeably in the U.S. market, though "E&O" is more common in some industries (real estate, insurance) and "professional liability" in others (healthcare, legal).

Can I lower my E&O insurance cost?

Five proven levers: (1) raise your deductible from $2,500 to $5,000 or $10,000 (saves 10–20%); (2) bundle E&O with general liability or cyber in a single policy (saves 10–15%); (3) maintain a clean 3-year claims history; (4) shop 3+ carriers every year (rates vary 20–30%); (5) use strong engagement contracts with clear scope, limitation of liability, and dispute resolution clauses — some carriers offer 5–10% credits for these.
