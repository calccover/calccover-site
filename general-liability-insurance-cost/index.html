How Much Does General Liability Insurance Cost in 2026?

General liability insurance is the foundation of every small business insurance program. Most small businesses pay between $500 and $1,500 per year for a $1M/$2M policy — but the range hides enormous variation by industry, state, revenue, and claims history. This guide breaks down exactly what you should pay in 2026.

By Emiliano · Last updated 2026-10-13 · 8 min read

The short answer: $500 to $1,500 per year

For a typical small business with under $1M in revenue, a clean 3-year claims history, and a low-to-medium risk industry class code, general liability costs:

Coverage LevelAnnual CostMonthly Equivalent
$1M / $2M (standard)$500–$1,500$42–$125
$2M / $4M (higher limits)$900–$2,400$75–$200
$1M / $2M + umbrella ($1M)$700–$2,300$58–$192

That's just the GL line. If you bundle GL with commercial property in a Business Owner's Policy (BOP), you'll pay $600–$2,500 total — often 10–25% less than buying them separately.

What drives your GL premium

  • Class code — the base rate your industry generates. Ranges from $0.30 to $12+ per $100 of revenue-equivalent
  • Revenue — higher revenue = higher exposure = higher premium (roughly linear above $500K)
  • State — Northeast and coastal states cost 40–60% more than Mountain West
  • Claims history — one GL claim can raise your premium 15–40% for 3 years
  • Coverage limits — going from $1M/$2M to $2M/$4M raises premium 20–35%
  • Deductible — going from $500 to $2,500 cuts premium 10–15%

How to lower your GL premium

  1. Verify your NCCI class code. Misclassification is the #1 reason small businesses overpay — sometimes by 30–50%. Ask your broker to confirm it in writing.
  2. Shop 3+ carriers every year. Identical coverage varies 20–30% between carriers. Loyalty is expensive.
  3. Raise your deductible. $500 → $2,500 saves 10–15% annually.
  4. Bundle into a BOP. GL + property in one policy saves 10–25%.
  5. Pay annually. Avoids 3–8% in installment fees.

Frequently asked questions

How much does general liability insurance cost?

Most small businesses pay $500–$1,500 per year for a $1M/$2M general liability policy. Low-risk industries like consulting and retail average $500–$900. Higher-risk industries like construction and restaurants pay $2,000–$6,000. State variation swings the total 40–60%.

Is $1M enough general liability coverage?

$1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate is the standard limit and satisfies most commercial leases and B2B contracts. Businesses with higher liability exposure — contractors, manufacturers, anyone with frequent public interaction — should consider $2M/$4M or add an umbrella policy for $200–$800 per year.

Do I need general liability insurance if I work from home?

Yes, if you have client visits, provide advice, or sell products. Homeowners policies exclude business liability entirely. Even a home-based consultant should carry $1M/$2M general liability, which costs $40–$80 per month for most low-risk industries.

What is the cheapest general liability insurance?

For low-risk businesses, the cheapest GL comes from direct-to-business carriers like Hiscox, Next Insurance, and Thimble. Rates range from $30–$70 per month for $1M/$2M coverage in a clerical or retail class code with a clean claims history.

How do I lower my general liability premium?

Five levers: (1) verify your NCCI class code; (2) raise your deductible from $500 to $2,500 (saves 10–15%); (3) shop 3+ carriers every year (rates vary 20–30%); (4) bundle GL with property in a BOP (saves 10–25%); (5) pay annually instead of monthly (saves 3–8%).
