How Calccover calculates your estimate

Every calculator on this site uses a transparent formula built from publicly available insurance industry data. This page explains exactly how each calculation works — no black boxes, no hidden assumptions.

The core principle: transparent formulas

Commercial insurance pricing is complex, but it's not secret. Carriers use rate factors that are published by rating bureaus like ISO (Verisk) and summarized in public research from the Insurance Information Institute (III) and the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC). Our calculators use those same public factors to give you a realistic estimate in seconds.

We don't have access to your actual claims history, credit-based insurance score, or the specific underwriting guidelines of any individual carrier — so our estimates are ballpark figures, not quotes. But they're grounded in the same data your broker will use.

Bottom line: our calculators tell you what a policy typically costs. For a binding price, you need a licensed broker to run your specific risk.

The general formula

Every commercial insurance calculator follows a similar structure:

ComponentWhat it does
Base rateThe starting premium for your industry class, taken from published rate tables
Revenue factorScales the premium up as your revenue (exposure) grows
Employee factorAdjusts for headcount — more employees means more exposure
State factorMultiplies for local tort environment and regulatory costs
Coverage limit factorAdjusts for the limit you choose ($1M, $2M, etc.)

The final premium is roughly: Base rate × Revenue factor × Employee factor × State factor × Limit factor. Each calculator on the site tailors these factors to its specific line of insurance.

Where the numbers come from

  • Insurance Information Institute (III) — annual industry reports on commercial premium averages
  • NAIC — state-by-state regulatory filings and market data
  • ISO / Verisk — publicly summarized commercial rating factors
  • Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) — injury rates by industry, used for workers' comp calculations
  • State insurance departments — published rate filings for workers' comp and commercial auto

How to verify a calculation yourself

You don't have to trust this page. Here's how to sanity-check any of our estimates:

The 3-step verification

  1. Run a calculation on any calculator (e.g., General Liability).
  2. Open the "How this calculator works" details block below the tool — it shows the exact formula used.
  3. Cross-check the result against III industry averages, which are free to read at iii.org.

If our estimate is outside the published average range for your industry, one of the inputs (usually state factor or revenue) is likely off. Adjust and try again.

What our calculators do NOT do

  • They don't see your claims history. A single prior claim can change a real premium by 20–40%.
  • They don't apply credit-based insurance scores. Many states allow carriers to use these; we don't have access to yours.
  • They don't account for policy endorsements. Additional insureds, waivers of subrogation, and special coverages all add cost.
  • They don't replace a broker. A licensed broker can often find you a better rate than the average we estimate.

How often we update the data

We review our rate factors quarterly and update them when new III or NAIC reports are published. The "Last updated" date on this page reflects our most recent review.
