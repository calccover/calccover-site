How much does a business interruption insurance quote cost?

A business interruption insurance quote typically ranges from $500 to $3,000 per year for small to medium-sized businesses. The quote depends on your industry risk profile, revenue volume, geography, and the length of the indemnity period you select. Most standalone BI policies are actually written as endorsements to a commercial property policy or bundled inside a Business Owner's Policy (BOP), which is usually the cheapest path to coverage.

Businesses in high-risk zones or sectors vulnerable to prolonged physical damage closures pay higher rates per thousand dollars of coverage. What factors affect your business interruption quote?

Several core risk variables influence the final premium quoted for your business interruption policy:

  • Industry classification — hospitality and manufacturing face higher loss multipliers than professional consulting firms due to complex supply chains and specialized repair timelines.
  • Geographic location — rates rise in regions with higher disaster frequency (hurricanes, wildfires, severe winter storms).
  • Indemnity period — the number of months your policy covers lost income directly scales both the coverage limit and the premium.
  • Coverage limit — calculated as (monthly net income + monthly continuing expenses) × indemnity months.
  • Deductible and waiting period — higher deductibles and longer waiting periods reduce annual premium.
  • Claims history — a clean 3-year loss run qualifies for preferred pricing tiers.
  • Disaster recovery plan — documented mitigation protocols help negotiate lower rates.

How much coverage do you need?

Most small businesses need a coverage limit equal to 6–12 months of gross revenue, or 12 months of net income plus continuing fixed expenses. The right number depends on how long it would realistically take to:

  • Repair or replace the damaged property
  • Rebuild your customer base after reopening
  • Get back to pre-loss revenue levels

Restaurants, retail, and manufacturing typically choose 12 months. Professional services firms often choose 6 months because they can operate remotely during many types of closures.

Frequently asked questions

What does business interruption insurance actually cover?

It covers lost net income and continuing normal operating expenses — such as rent, utilities, employee payroll, and loan payments — while your business operations are suspended due to a covered property loss.

Is business interruption insurance required by law?

No federal, state, or local laws legally mandate business interruption insurance. However, commercial landlords or commercial lenders financing your equipment and real estate will frequently require it as part of your lease or loan agreement.

Does business interruption cover losses from pandemics or viral outbreaks?

Standard business interruption policies explicitly exclude losses caused by viruses, bacteria, and global pandemics unless specifically endorsed, which is exceptionally rare and expensive in current insurance markets.
