How Much Does Professional Liability Insurance Cost?

Professional liability insurance, commonly known as Errors and Omissions (E&O) insurance, varies significantly depending on your specific line of work, business size, and exposure limits. On average, small to mid-sized businesses can expect to pay anywhere from $1,000 to $5,000 annually for a policy carrying a $1,000,000 limit. Solo practitioners and low-risk consultants often secure coverage at the lower end of that spectrum, while high-stakes professions such as law, accounting, and specialized engineering routinely see higher baseline costs due to the severe financial damages associated with professional mistakes.

Beyond baseline premiums, your choice of deductible (self-insured retention) and aggregate policy limits heavily dictate final pricing. Selecting a higher deductible can lower your ongoing monthly or annual premiums, but requires careful internal risk assessment in the event of an unexpected claim or legal defense cost.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

Underwriters analyze several critical data points when underwriting a professional liability policy. Industry classification remains one of the heaviest weighted elements; professions with a high frequency of litigation or severe client financial loss face elevated rates. Your annual gross revenue is another major driver, as higher billings generally indicate a larger volume of completed work, more active client contracts, and statistically greater exposure to potential disputes.

Additionally, the number of professional employees and subcontractors you manage influences your quote, since more personnel introduces greater human error potential. Regional litigiousness and state-specific legal environments also play a quiet yet decisive role in pricing your commercial coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does professional liability insurance actually cover?

Professional liability insurance protects your business against claims of financial loss resulting from alleged negligence, errors, omissions, or failure to deliver professional services as promised. This covers legal defense costs, settlements, and judgments, even if the underlying claim lacks merit.

Is professional liability insurance legally required?

Unlike commercial auto or workers' compensation insurance, professional liability is rarely mandated by state statute. However, many enterprise clients explicitly require proof of E&O insurance in their vendor contracts before signing service agreements or onboarding your firm.

What is the difference between general liability and professional liability?

General liability insurance covers physical risks such as bodily injury, property damage, and advertising injury occurring on your premises or job site. Professional liability exclusively covers financial losses stemming from professional mistakes, bad advice, or missed project deadlines.
