How much does landscape insurance cost?

Landscape insurance costs $1,800–$6,500 per year for most small landscaping and lawn care businesses. That total breaks into three main coverages:

  • General liability: $1,200–$3,000 per year — protects against property damage to customer lawns and third-party injuries
  • Workers' compensation (Class 0042): ~$0.065 per dollar of payroll — for crew injuries, tree-trimming accidents, and equipment-related injuries
  • Commercial auto: ~$1,800 per vehicle per year — covers trucks hauling trailers and equipment between job sites

High-cost states like California, New York, New Jersey, Florida, Connecticut, and Massachusetts add 30–40% to the total. Our 50-state cost analysis shows workers' comp alone can vary 5x between the cheapest and most expensive state for the same payroll.

What factors affect your landscape insurance cost?

Insurance underwriters look closely at several operational risk metrics when calculating your final premium quotes:

  • Annual revenue — primary indicator of exposure, reflecting job volume and public interaction
  • Payroll and employee count — directly scales workers' comp premiums; landscaping and tree trimming carry inherent physical hazards
  • Geographic location — state-specific litigation climates, weather risks, and labor regulations add 30–40% in high-cost states
  • Equipment inventory value — drives inland marine and property coverage additions
  • Commercial vehicle fleet size — each truck or trailer adds auto premium
  • Claims history — a clean 3-year loss run qualifies for preferred pricing tiers
  • Safety program and documentation — formal training and safety certificates can reduce workers' comp 5–15%

What insurance does a landscaping business need?

Most landscaping companies need a stack of coverages. Priority list:

  • General liability — foundation for every landscaping business. Most commercial contracts require $1M/$2M minimum.
  • Workers' compensation — mandatory in almost every state once you have W-2 employees. Class code 0042 is standard.
  • Commercial auto — required for any vehicle used for business. Personal auto excludes business use.
  • Inland marine — covers tools and equipment in transit between job sites. Standard property policies exclude off-premises equipment.
  • Commercial umbrella — additional liability on top of GL and auto. Cheap ($200–$800/year) and recommended for high-traffic operations.
  • Cyber liability — if you take card payments or store customer data.

Frequently asked questions

How much does landscape insurance cost?

Landscape insurance costs $1,800–$6,500 per year for most small landscaping and lawn care businesses. General liability runs $1,200–$3,000, workers' compensation under class code 0042 scales with payroll at roughly $0.065 per dollar of payroll, and commercial auto averages $1,800 per vehicle per year. High-cost states add 30–40% to the total.

Why is landscaping insurance more expensive than typical office business insurance?

Landscaping involves heavy machinery, chemical applications, commercial vehicles, and physical labor on third-party properties. The exposure to property damage, employee injuries, and auto accidents drives rates higher than standard office operations — typically 3–5x higher for the same revenue.

Is general liability insurance enough for my lawn care business?

Generally no. General liability protects against third-party bodily injury and property damage, but it does not cover your own employees' injuries or your work trucks and mowers. Most lawn care businesses need general liability, workers' comp, and commercial auto in addition to a BOP.

How can I lower my landscape insurance cost?

Five levers: (1) verify your NCCI class code is 0042 — misclassification is the top overpayment driver; (2) implement documented safety training; (3) maintain clean driving records for all crew members; (4) bundle policies with a single carrier (10–25% savings); (5) raise deductibles from $500 to $2,500 (10–15% savings).

Do I need insurance to bid on commercial landscaping contracts?

Yes. Most commercial property managers, HOAs, and government contracts require a Certificate of Insurance (COI) showing minimum coverage before you can bid. Typical requirements are $1M/$2M general liability, $1M commercial auto, and workers' comp.

What NCCI class code applies to landscaping businesses?

The primary NCCI class code for lawn care and landscaping is 0042 (Lawn Care or Landscaping Services). Tree trimming and removal may fall under different codes depending on your state. Getting the code right matters — misclassification is the most common reason landscaping businesses overpay, sometimes by 30–50%.

Does landscape insurance cover damage to customer property?

Yes. General liability covers third-party property damage caused by your operations — for example, a mower that throws a rock through a customer's window, or a truck that backs into a customer's garage. Coverage is subject to your deductible and policy limits.
