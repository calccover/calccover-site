Beauty & Wellness Insurance: Complete 2026 Guide

Beauty and wellness businesses face unique risks: chemical exposure, client injuries, and equipment damage. Whether you run a salon, spa, or personal training studio, this hub covers what you need.

By Emiliano · Last updated 2026-10-16 · 10 min read

What insurance does a beauty & wellness insurance business need?

Most beauty & wellness insurance businesses need a stack of coverages. The priority list:

  • General Liability — Client slip-and-falls, property damage, and third-party injuries at your location.
  • Professional Liability — Chemical burns, allergic reactions, botched styling, or training injuries. Essential for anyone providing direct services to clients.
  • Commercial Property — Chairs, equipment, inventory, tenant improvements. Covers fire, theft, and weather damage.
  • Workers' Compensation — Employee injuries (burns, cuts, repetitive motion). Mandatory with W-2 employees in almost every state.
  • Business Interruption — Lost income during a covered closure (fire, storm, etc.).
  • Cyber Liability — If you take card payments or store client information.

Frequently asked questions

How much does salon insurance cost?

Salon insurance costs $1,500–$4,500 per year for most single-location salons. A small barbershop pays $1,200–$3,000; a day spa pays $2,500–$7,000. Cost depends on number of chairs, services offered, and state.

Do salons need professional liability insurance?

Yes, if you offer chemical treatments, hair coloring, or skincare services. Professional liability covers chemical burns, allergic reactions, and claims that your services caused harm. State licensing boards often require proof of coverage.

Do personal trainers need insurance?

Yes. Even solo personal trainers need professional liability if they train clients. Most gyms and studios require proof of $1M coverage before allowing independent trainers to operate.

Do I need workers' comp for stylists who rent booths?

Booth renters are typically classified as independent contractors, not employees. You don't need to cover them under your workers' comp — they need their own policy. But if a state reclassifies them as employees (California AB5 is strict on this), you can be retroactively liable.

What insurance does a day spa need?

Day spas typically need GL, professional liability, commercial property, and workers' comp. If you offer medical-adjacent services (Botox, laser treatments), you may need additional medical malpractice coverage.

How much does personal trainer insurance cost?

Personal trainer insurance costs $500–$1,200 per year for a solo trainer. Group coverage for multiple trainers runs $300–$600 per trainer per year. E&O coverage is the core coverage; GL is often bundled.
