How Much Does Liquor Liability Insurance Cost?

Liquor liability insurance costs between $1,500 and $25,000+ per year depending on the establishment. A restaurant selling beer and wine might pay $1,500–$3,500. A busy nightclub can pay $15,000–$50,000. Premiums scale with alcohol sales, occupancy, and claims history.

What Factors Affect Your Premium?

1. Establishment Type

Restaurants with food-primary sales have the lowest rates. Bars, taverns, and nightclubs pay more because they have higher alcohol-to-food ratios and more late-night incidents.

2. Alcohol Revenue

Higher alcohol sales means more drinks served and more exposure. Insurers typically use alcohol revenue as the primary rating basis.

3. Occupancy

Larger venues have more potential for incidents. Capacity directly affects the premium — a 200-person venue pays more than an 80-person one.

4. Security Staff

Having trained security staff reduces premiums by 10–20%. Insurers view it as a significant risk mitigation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is liquor liability required?

Yes, in nearly every state. Anyone who sells or serves alcohol is legally required to carry liquor liability insurance. Some states require a minimum coverage amount before issuing a liquor license.

What does liquor liability cover?

Claims arising from serving alcohol to intoxicated or underage persons. Covers legal defense, settlements, and judgments. It also covers "dram shop" liability — claims by third parties injured by an intoxicated customer.

What if I only sell beer and wine?

You still need liquor liability insurance in most states. The premium is lower because of the reduced alcohol content and lower risk of intoxication.
