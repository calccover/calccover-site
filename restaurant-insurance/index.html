Restaurant Insurance: Complete 2026 Guide

Restaurant insurance is more complex than most small business coverage. Kitchens, alcohol service, food-borne illness, and high foot traffic all create unique exposures. This hub covers what every restaurant needs.

By Emiliano · Last updated 2026-10-16 · 10 min read

$2,000 – $20,000

What insurance does a restaurant insurance business need?

Most restaurant insurance businesses need a stack of coverages. The priority list:

  • General Liability — Customer slip-and-falls, food-borne illness claims, third-party injuries. The foundation coverage for every restaurant.
  • Liquor Liability — Required by state licensing boards if you serve alcohol. Standard GL excludes all alcohol-related claims.
  • Commercial Property — Kitchen equipment, furniture, tenant improvements, inventory. Covers fire, theft, and weather damage.
  • Business Interruption — Lost income if a covered loss (fire, storm, etc.) forces you to close temporarily.
  • Workers' Compensation — Kitchen burns, cuts, slips, and repetitive motion injuries. Mandatory with W-2 employees.
  • Commercial Auto — If you offer delivery, you need commercial auto. Personal auto excludes business use.
  • Cyber Liability — If you take card payments or store customer data — POS breaches are common targets.

Frequently asked questions

How much does restaurant insurance cost?

Restaurant insurance costs $2,000–$20,000+ per year depending on type. A small cafe pays $2,000–$4,500; a casual dining restaurant pays $5,000–$9,000; a bar or nightclub pays $9,500–$20,000+. The biggest cost drivers are alcohol service, seating capacity, and revenue.

Do restaurants need liquor liability insurance?

Yes, any establishment that sells, serves, or distributes alcohol needs liquor liability coverage. Standard general liability policies specifically exclude all alcohol-related claims. Even a cafe serving only craft beer needs it.

Is business interruption insurance required for restaurants?

Not legally, but strongly recommended. Restaurants face high closure risk from fire, storms, and equipment failure. Business interruption covers lost income and continuing expenses during a covered closure.

How much does workers' comp cost for a restaurant?

Restaurant workers' comp typically runs $1.00–$3.00 per $100 of payroll depending on state and job class. Kitchen staff pay more than front-of-house. For a restaurant with $300,000 in payroll, expect $3,000–$9,000 per year.

What is the cheapest restaurant insurance?

A small BOP bundling GL and property is the cheapest option for low-risk cafes, averaging $2,000–$4,500 per year. Adding liquor liability for a full bar adds $1,000–$6,000+ depending on alcohol sales volume.

Do I need insurance before opening my restaurant?

Yes. Most landlords require a Certificate of Insurance showing GL and property coverage before signing a lease. State liquor boards require liquor liability proof before issuing a liquor license. You cannot legally open in most states without coverage.
