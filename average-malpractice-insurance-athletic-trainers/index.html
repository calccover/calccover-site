Average Cost of Malpractice Insurance for Athletic Trainers

Athletic trainers pay between $100 and $600 per year for malpractice (professional liability) insurance, depending on coverage limits, employer, and state. That's one of the lowest malpractice premiums in healthcare — but the protection is essential, because a single claim against an athletic trainer typically costs $15,000+ in defense alone. Here's the complete 2026 cost breakdown.

By Emiliano · Last updated 2026-10-14 · 9 min read

What drives the cost up or down

Factors that lower your premium

  • Group purchasing — buying through a professional association (NATA, state chapters, university programs) saves 20–40%
  • Claims-free history — 5+ years with no claims qualifies for preferred tiers (10–25% credit)
  • Continuing education in risk management — some carriers discount 5–10% for CE credits in documentation, patient safety, or ethics
  • Higher deductible — going from $0 to $1,000 saves 10–15%
  • Occurrence policy vs. claims-made — occurrence is slightly more expensive upfront but cheaper over the long run (no tail coverage needed when you change carriers)

Factors that raise your premium

  • Independent practice ownership — if you own a clinic, gym, or sports medicine practice, you need higher limits and more coverages (adds $200–$400/year)
  • Treatment of minors without supervision — youth sports coverage adds 15–25% to premium
  • Manual therapy techniques — spinal manipulation, dry needling, and other techniques that go beyond standard scope require endorsements ($50–$150 extra)
  • Prior claims — one malpractice claim doubles your premium for 3–5 years
  • High-risk sub-specialty — athletic trainers working with high-contact sports (football, hockey, rugby) pay more than those working with endurance athletes

Who actually needs malpractice insurance?

Every certified athletic trainer should carry professional liability coverage, but the specific need depends on your practice setting:

Employed by a school or university

Many employers provide professional liability coverage as a benefit, but always confirm in writing. Some policies have coverage gaps for events like:

  • Unpaid volunteer work at outside athletic events
  • Consulting or private practice work done outside your employment
  • Coverage for treating athletes who aren't students or employees

An individual policy of $100–$200/year closes those gaps.

Employed by a hospital or clinic

Hospitals and clinics typically carry institutional professional liability that covers athletic trainers as employees. But if you're classified as a contractor or 1099 worker, you are almost certainly not covered by the institutional policy and need your own.

Independent practice or sports medicine clinic owner

You need professional liability plus general liability, commercial property, and workers' comp if you have staff. This is a higher-cost scenario: $800–$2,500 per year total for a small athletic training practice.

High school, club, or travel sports coverage

If you provide athletic training services to youth or amateur sports clubs as a contractor, you need your own policy. Employer coverage does not extend to these settings. This is where the "average" athletic trainer is most likely to have a gap — and where a single claim could exhaust the limits of a standard $1M/$3M policy.

What malpractice insurance actually covers

Standard professional liability coverage for athletic trainers includes:

  • Defense costs — attorney fees and expert witness costs, typically paid on top of the liability limit
  • Damages — up to the policy limit for claims of negligence or improper treatment
  • HIPAA-related regulatory defense — coverage for OCR investigations and civil penalties
  • Deposition and disciplinary hearings — coverage for licensing board investigations
  • Treatment of minors — if disclosed and endorsed on the policy
  • Good Samaritan coverage — emergency care provided outside your normal practice setting

What it does not cover:

  • General liability — slip-and-fall, property damage at your facility. Requires a separate policy or BOP.
  • Workers' compensation — injuries to your own employees. Requires a separate policy.
  • Commercial auto — accidents while driving to athletic events. Requires a commercial auto policy.
  • Cyber liability — data breaches affecting athlete health records. Requires a separate policy.

Where to buy malpractice insurance for athletic trainers

Five main channels, ranked by cost:

  1. Professional association group programs — NATA and state chapters (like the Hawaii Athletic Trainers Association) negotiate group rates that save 20–40%. Usually the cheapest option.
  2. Direct carriers — companies like Proliability, CPH & Associates, and HPSO sell direct to athletic trainers with quick online quotes.
  3. Independent insurance brokers — shop your coverage to multiple carriers. Best for athletic trainers with complex needs (own practice, multiple states, unique scope).
  4. Employer-sponsored programs — if your employer offers coverage as a benefit, take it. But verify the scope of coverage in writing.
  5. Online marketplaces — digital-first options like Next Insurance and Thimble are convenient but may not have the athletic-trainer-specific endorsements you need.

Recommendation: Start with the NATA-affiliated group program, then compare against Proliability and one broker quote. Three quotes is enough to find the best rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does malpractice insurance cost for an athletic trainer?

Individual athletic trainer professional liability insurance costs $100–$200 per year for a $1M/$3M policy. Higher limits ($2M/$4M) run $200–$400 per year. Coverage for athletic trainers who also do personal training or own a practice runs $300–$600 per year. Group policies for schools or clinics cost $50–$150 per athletic trainer per year when purchased together.

Is malpractice insurance required for athletic trainers?

Short answer: Requirements vary by state and setting. Many states require proof of professional liability coverage to maintain BOC certification and state licensure. Most school districts, universities, and clinical employers require it as a condition of employment.

Even where not legally mandated, coverage is strongly recommended because the average malpractice claim against an athletic trainer runs $15,000–$50,000 in defense costs alone.

What does athletic trainer malpractice insurance cover?

Standard professional liability coverage for athletic trainers includes: defense costs for claims of negligence or improper treatment, coverage for injuries sustained by athletes under your care, HIPAA-related regulatory defense, coverage for treatment of minors (if disclosed), and coverage for deposition and disciplinary hearings. It does not cover general liability, commercial auto, or workers' compensation.

Do athletic trainers need both malpractice and general liability insurance?

Short answer: Yes, in most cases. Professional liability covers claims that your professional services caused harm. General liability covers third-party claims unrelated to the quality of care — for example, an athlete slipping in your training facility. If you own a clinic or gym, you need both.

If you are employed by a school or clinic, the employer typically provides GL but not always professional liability.

Does the employer's insurance cover an athletic trainer?

Short answer: Sometimes, but not always. If your employer is a hospital, university, or school district, they may provide professional liability coverage. But many employers require athletic trainers to carry individual policies as a condition of employment.

If you practice as a contractor, independent consultant, or in a private capacity, the employer's policy almost certainly does not cover you. Always confirm in writing.

What is the difference between claims-made and occurrence malpractice coverage?

Short answer: Occurrence policies cover incidents that happen during the policy period, regardless of when the claim is filed. Claims-made policies only cover claims filed while the policy is active. For athletic trainers, occurrence policies are generally recommended because claims can be filed years after an injury.

If you choose claims-made, you will need to purchase extended reporting period (tail) coverage if you switch carriers or retire.

How do I lower my malpractice insurance premium as an athletic trainer?

Five levers: (1) bundle professional liability with general liability if you own a practice (10–20% savings); (2) join a professional association that has a group program (NATA-affiliated programs often save 10–30%); (3) increase your deductible from $0 to $1,000 (10–15% savings); (4) take continuing education on risk management (some carriers discount 5–10%); (5) maintain a clean claims history — one claim can double your premium for 3–5 years.
