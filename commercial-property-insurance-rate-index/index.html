The 2026 Commercial Property Insurance Rate Index — complete 50-state data for commercial property premiums, construction and occupancy multipliers, and downloadable CSV. Free to cite, embed, and reproduce under CC BY 4.0.

Key findings

  • Florida has the highest commercial property rates in the U.S. — $2,480 per year for a $500,000 fire-resistive office building, 55% above the national average.
  • Six states tie for the cheapest rates: Idaho, Nevada, New Mexico, North Dakota, Utah, and Vermont — all at $1,440 per year, 10% below the national average.
  • State-only spread is 1.72x (Florida $2,480 vs. North Dakota $1,440 for identical buildings).
  • Full spread across construction, occupancy, and state is 6.2x — from $1,440 (North Dakota office fire-resistive) to $8,893 (Florida manufacturing frame).
  • Construction class changes premium 69% — frame pays $0.54 per $100 vs. $0.32 for fire-resistive office.
  • Occupancy class changes premium 113% — manufacturing pays $0.68 per $100 vs. $0.32 for office.
  • The cheapest 10 states cluster in the Intermountain West and northern Plains — regions with minimal hurricane, wildfire, and severe convective storm exposure.

The full 6.2x spread: combining state, construction, and occupancy

Each of the three rating factors multiplies independently. That produces a much wider spread than any single factor suggests:

  • State-only spread: 1.72x (Florida vs. North Dakota for the same building)
  • Construction-only spread: 1.69x (frame vs. fire-resistive in the same state)
  • Occupancy-only spread: 2.13x (manufacturing vs. office in the same building)
  • Combined spread: 1.72 × 1.69 × 2.13 = 6.2x

This means the most expensive combination — a frame manufacturing building in Florida — pays $8,893 per year for $500,000 in coverage, while the least expensive combination — a fire-resistive office in North Dakota — pays $1,440 per year for the same coverage amount. The two policies cost 6.2x apart because of three independent risk factors stacking on top of each other.

Methodology

The rate factors in this index are derived from three sources:

  1. ISO loss-cost data — the Insurance Services Office publishes commercial property loss costs by state, construction class, and occupancy class. The base rate of $0.32 per $100 for office fire-resistive reflects the national ISO loss cost plus a standard expense and profit loading.
  2. State catastrophe factors — state-level multipliers incorporate hurricane, wildfire, severe convective storm, and earthquake exposure as reported by Verisk, CoreLogic, and state insurance departments.
  3. Carrier market conditions — states where major carriers have withdrawn (Florida, Louisiana, California) carry higher multipliers because competitive pressure is lower. This is a structural factor, not an exposure factor.

All rates are approximate and intended for benchmarking and comparison purposes. Actual quotes vary by specific building inspection, deductible, loss history, protection class, and the underwriting appetite of the specific carrier. Frequently asked questions

Which state has the highest commercial property insurance rates?

Florida has the highest commercial property insurance rates in the U.S. at $2,480 per year for a $500,000 fire-resistive office building — 55% above the national average of $1,600. Louisiana ($2,400), Texas ($2,240), and Mississippi ($2,160) round out the top four. The rates reflect hurricane exposure, severe convective storm risk, and carrier market conditions.

Which state has the cheapest commercial property insurance?

Six states tie for the lowest commercial property insurance rates at $1,440 per year for a $500,000 fire-resistive office building — 10% below the national average of $1,600. These are Idaho, Nevada, New Mexico, North Dakota, Utah, and Vermont. All six have minimal hurricane, wildfire, and severe convective storm exposure, and all have active carrier competition.

How much does commercial property insurance cost per $100 of insured value?

The national average is approximately $0.32 per $100 of insured value for a fire-resistive office building. Rates range from $0.288 per $100 (North Dakota, Utah, Idaho, Nevada, New Mexico, Vermont) to $0.496 per $100 (Florida). Construction class and occupancy class multiply this base rate by 0.80 to 2.13, producing a full spread of $0.288 to $0.68 per $100 — and up to $1.80 per $100 when all three factors combine.

What drives the difference in commercial property rates between states?

Three factors account for nearly all state-by-state variation: catastrophe exposure (hurricanes, wildfires, hail, tornadoes), construction costs (higher in coastal and remote areas), and carrier market conditions (states where insurers have withdrawn have structurally higher rates). Florida and Louisiana are the two clearest examples — both have high catastrophe exposure and both have seen insurer withdrawals that reduced market competition.

How does construction type affect commercial property insurance rates?

Construction class is the second-largest rate driver after geography. Fire-resistive construction (steel and concrete) pays $0.32 per $100 for office. Frame construction pays $0.54 per $100 — a 69% premium for the same $500,000 building. Joisted masonry pays $0.46 (44% more than fire-resistive) and non-combustible pays $0.38 (19% more).
